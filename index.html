About

I'm a machine-learning researcher who reads deep learning as applied mathematics — and mathematics as a source of new learning algorithms.

My full name is Seyed MohammadHossein Hosseini Dastjae — not unlike Albus Percival Wulfric Brian Dumbledore — but I mostly go by Mamad. I hold a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering from the University of Isfahan, where my undergraduate work centered on statistical inference and generative modeling, alongside projects across computer vision, graph neural networks, and natural language processing.

What draws me is the way pure mathematics keeps generating ideas for machine learning. The stochastic-calculus reading of diffusion and flow models; homeomorphism interpolation carried out by Neural ODEs; the deep kinship between normalizing flows and the theory of differential equations — these are a few of the threads I keep pulling. I want to push further into more abstract structure: to find stochastic processes better suited to generative modeling, analytically and topologically.

Right now that means diffusion and score-based SDEs for structured and time-series data, density estimation and variational inference, and the geometry and topology of latent spaces. If any of this overlaps with what you're thinking about, I'd be glad to hear from you.

Currently: researching generative models & stochastic processes. Open to graduate & research opportunities.

Research Universe

Six threads through one question

I study machine learning from the vantage point of pure mathematics — asking which analytic, geometric, and topological structures make learning possible, and how those structures translate into models that generate, infer, and reason. Six threads organize the work.

01

Generative Models

Learning to sample from complex, high-dimensional distributions — from denoising diffusion and implicit samplers to autoregressive and latent-variable models.

How do we turn noise into structure? I work across the modern generative landscape: score-based diffusion, deterministic samplers, normalizing flows, adversarial and energy-based models, and variational autoencoders — with a bias toward the underlying probabilistic and dynamical principles rather than architecture alone.

Probabilistic DiffusionImplicit Diffusion (DDIM)Normalizing FlowsVariational Autoencoder
02

Stochastic Processes & SDEs

Reading generative modeling through stochastic calculus: diffusion as a stochastic differential equation, and the search for processes better suited to data.

The score-based view recasts generation as integrating a reverse-time SDE. I am interested in which stochastic processes — beyond the standard Brownian scaffold — are analytically and topologically better matched to real data, particularly structured and time-series data.

Probabilistic DiffusionImplicit Diffusion (DDIM)Normalizing Flows
03

Density Estimation & Inference

Estimating and approximating distributions — variational inference, expectation–maximization, and exact-likelihood models.

From mean-field and automatic-differentiation variational inference to the EM algorithm and change-of-variables likelihoods, I study how we quantify uncertainty and fit densities to data, and where these approximations succeed or break down.

Expectation–MaximizationMean-Field Variational InferenceAutomatic Differentiation VINormalizing Flows
04

Topological & Geometric ML

Bringing topology and geometry to bear on learning: persistent homology, manifold structure, and homeomorphism interpolation via Neural ODEs.

Data has shape. I use tools from algebraic topology — persistent homology and beyond — to characterize that shape, and study continuous-depth models (Neural ODEs) as homeomorphisms that deform latent space while preserving topological invariants.

Persistent HomologyNormalizing Flows
05

Graph Neural Networks

Learning on relational structure — spectral convolution and attention over graphs.

Much of the world is relational. I study message passing, graph convolution, and graph attention as principled ways to learn representations on non-Euclidean domains, and how spectral and geometric perspectives illuminate their behavior.

Graph Convolutional NetworksGraph Attention Networks
06

Mathematical Foundations of Deep Learning

The through-line: differential equations, stochastic calculus, and topology as the grammar behind deep models.

Across all of the above sits one conviction — that abstract mathematics is a generative source of ideas for learning, not just an after-the-fact justification. Flows and differential equations, diffusion and stochastic calculus, latent spaces and topology: I want to keep pulling these threads.
